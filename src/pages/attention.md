I had something very interesting happen to me tonight.

I was watching _Summer Palace_ by Lou Ye. When it ended, I felt like telling
the person I'm seeing about it, and naturally that provoked the urge to grab my
phone. But instead, I figured I could take my audiopager, and record a message
to her there. I spent around five minutes lying on the couch and voicing my
thoughts about the film I'd just seen. When I finished, I felt such a peace and
quiet... After a while, I washed the dishes and played a bit of the movie's
soundtrack that was still in my head on the piano. Now, half an hour later, I'm
listening to music and writing this down, and I still feel that same peace and
quiet. It's such a rare feeling to have!

I'm certain that, had I taken my phone at the beginning, I would've gone down a
different path, one of the many that the device offers me at any point. I
might've sent that message about the film, but it wouldn't have been that
thoughtful of a reflection, and I might not have even gotten to sending it. (We
don't even say write anymore, just send!) I'm certain I would have checked
other random bits of information, because my mind continually craves them when
I'm on my phone. I go back and forth between different apps, and it seems I'm
unable to put the device down.

Is it better to do the dishes than to reply to an incoming message, or check
some reels? Although your mom might say so, I'm not here to argue the benefits
of a clean sink. Instead, I want to share how good it felt to experience that
quiet, uninterrupted flow of awareness. For sure, the film I saw helped evoke
such a state of mind. But then it lingered, and I lingered in the dream of a
present moment that didn't end because it didn't get interrupted. It made me
realize I don't want to give that up -- and how many times I *do* give that up
every day.

Where is that feeling normally? How often is it possible for me to be in that
locus of my mind, without experiencing the anxiety of a lack of incoming
information? Do I really want to chain my mind and body to this constant feed
of data, which my phone provides ever so willingly? The answer is obviously no,
but it's very hard to keep remembering that. (And phones are, at the end of the
day, very useful!)

We've grown so accustomed to checking our phones at *every* spare moment that
it is really, really hard to fully appreciate how weird and compulsive that
behaviour is. But try to witness, in the next ten minutes or so, how often you
feel like doing so, and how strong that feeling is. Isn't it kinda strange,
this non-stop desire for something to tell us what to think about next? (If
you're on your computer, try this with the act of opening a new tab!)

It's not like ditching your phone will make you walk around like a Zen Buddhist
monk the entire day. There will be many moments where your mind is racing,
distracted, anxious, wanting a break... Still, it feels obvious that there
should be devices available to us that provide those essential functions while
minimizing, rather than maximizing, how often our attention is hijacked. This
is what I'd like audiopager to do, the voice-message device I'm building: it
provides a simple communication link to a handful of people, free from
distraction. Because it is a beautiful thing to experience your attention
flowing the way it wants to, without interruption.
